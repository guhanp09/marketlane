# Product brief

## User and decision
A founder of a two-sided creative-services marketplace must choose an initial market scope and monetization approach.
Sparse supply can make a broad launch look impressive while buyers fail to find available services. This model turns
that hypothesis into explicit assumptions; it is not a substitute for user research.

## Decision criteria
Examine fulfilment and waiting time alongside contribution after modeled acquisition/support costs. Include inactive
suppliers when checking earnings concentration. Make buyer expiry and unmatched demand visible rather than excluding
these users from the denominator. Do not optimize platform fees without inspecting supplier feasibility.

## Baseline and alternative
Compare oldest-first greedy routing with a daily maximum-cardinality assignment. Both respect budgets, skills, quality
and capacity. The optimizer prioritizes current matching then surplus, and lacks inter-day planning. The baseline's
age priority can preserve jobs that would otherwise expire; this is why both were retained in evaluation.

## Research plan
Interview buyers about willingness to wait and desired service outcomes; interview sellers about availability,
reservation payouts, service duration and competing work. Validate demand arrival assumptions through a small pilot.
These interviews and the pilot are proposed, not completed. Replace assumptions with evidence before using the model
for acquisition spending or a launch decision.

## Release and monitoring
Audit the transaction ledger and unmatched demand, then monitor actual fill, time to fulfilment, repeat demand,
supplier retention and contribution. Revisit the policy if service quality, cancellations or strategic behavior change.
